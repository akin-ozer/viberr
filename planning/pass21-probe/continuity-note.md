# Per-Task Continuity

In a governed delivery system, continuity belongs to the task, not to any one
person, agent, terminal session, or stretch of uninterrupted attention.

The task must preserve the intended outcome, current stage, assigned role,
decisions, validation evidence, and the next useful action. That record lets
work pause safely and resume without requiring someone to reconstruct the
project from scattered messages or imperfect recollection.

Interruptions are ordinary: a session ends, a hand-off occurs, a reviewer
finds an issue, or a higher-priority task briefly takes precedence. Good
continuity makes those events changes in custody rather than losses of intent.

It also creates accountability. A new contributor can distinguish completed
work from proposed work, see what has actually been checked, and avoid
repeating a decision that was already recorded.

After an interruption, the canonical task file is the proper place to begin.
Its live snapshot states the goal, status, branch, recent timeline, and any
new instructions or constraints that arrived while the previous run was away.

Local memory is useful context, but it is private, partial, and time-bound.
Conversation history may be stale, while a checkout can contain changes whose
meaning is unclear without the task record. Neither is authoritative on its
own.

Re-anchoring prevents an agent from pursuing an obsolete goal, undoing a
resolved decision, or reporting progress against the wrong state. It turns a
restart into a deliberate continuation: inspect the shared record, compare it
with the working tree, then take the smallest action that advances the task.

That discipline keeps delivery legible across people and time. The result is
not merely resilient execution, but a trustworthy history of why the work
exists, what happened, and what should happen next.
